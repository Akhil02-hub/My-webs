01 Our Philosophy

We cook the way our grandmothers argued — with fire, patience, and very strong opinions.

Gulmohar began as a nine-table dining room above our family's spice shop in 1998. The tandoor was lit on the first evening and has never been allowed to die — the old hands say a tandoor remembers everything you have ever cooked in it. We still grind our own masalas at dawn, still source chillies from a single farm in Mathania, and still believe a guest is nothing short of divine.

What has changed is the grammar: classical Indian technique, spoken in a modern accent. Nothing imported. Nothing rushed. Nothing on the plate that our fire cannot improve.

04 The Keeper of the Flame
“My grandmother measured nothing. A palm of salt, a whisper of chilli, two hours of arguing. That is the recipe I still follow.”

Chef Aditya Rathore spent fifteen years in the fine kitchens of London and Singapore before coming home to the lake — and to the tandoor his grandfather lit in 1998. His cooking has been called many clever things by many clever critics. He describes it more simply: food that tastes like someone's memory.
